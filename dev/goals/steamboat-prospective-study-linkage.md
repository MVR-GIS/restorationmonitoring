## Candidate interpretations used for this test

These working interpretations make the record reviewable. They remain proposals until scientists and restoration practitioners review them.

| Concept | Candidate minimum meaning used here | Why it is distinct in this record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hypothesis | An attributed, independently reviewable proposition that states an expected relationship under stated conditions | SC-08 can be reviewed or revised without changing WQ-39, the Steamboat project, or the MS-03 findings that informed it |
| Study design | A versioned statement of how evidence is intended to bear on a hypothesis, including response, comparison, scope, collection and inference limits | MS-03 has a study design; no Steamboat mussel design has been located. A paper or dataset reference alone is insufficient |
| Planned evidence collection | A design-linked commitment or identified data source, with responsible contributor, method/timing reference and execution state | The user-reported baseline/construction context does not establish a dedicated mussel collection or a completed observation set |
| Implementation role | An attributed role that a specific planned or actual practice/feature has in a design, such as intervention, exposure, comparator, context or candidate opportunity | Stone protection can be a candidate local opportunity without claiming that a particular feature was installed or treated experimentally |
| Stewardship/review | The contributor or source responsible for a statement, the review state, and a time/version reference | The record can be handed forward while keeping assistant proposals separate from project or author assertions |

## Why this is a useful test

The record preserves a credible planning opportunity without converting relevance into evidence. A future scientist can find the question, the attributed proposition, relevant external study, source limits, local rationale and exact missing connections. A restoration practitioner can see that the anticipated benefit is ancillary, not a fabricated performance obligation. A program steward can identify the smallest additional artifacts needed to decide whether a formal study or evaluation is worthwhile.

The test also confirms that the proposed model must support multiple states at once: externally informed, locally relevant, not yet adopted, not yet designed, not yet linked to a specific implementation, and not yet evaluated. These are not mutually exclusive statuses and should not be collapsed into a single project status.

## Smallest follow-up artifacts, if the partnership chooses to advance the opportunity

1. A reviewed hypothesis/applicability statement that identifies conditions under which the external evidence may inform Steamboat.
2. A source-backed reference to the specific planned or installed restoration implementation and its condition/history.
3. An inventory of potentially relevant monitoring or research records, including owner, method, time/place coverage and access state.
4. A scoped design decision stating whether available evidence supports a local evaluation, a future study, or neither.

These are discovery and review artifacts, not new monitoring obligations or production data-entry requirements. Their creation, stewardship and timing remain partnership decisions.
