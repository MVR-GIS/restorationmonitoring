# Knowledge graph literature and project design assessment

Reviewed: 2026-10-16. The user's [KnowledgeGraphs collection](https://www.zotero.org/groups/4862069/usace_ecomodeling/collections/CLHU5MJG/collection) informed the project's expectations. Its public library interface displayed 89 items. This assessment screens the visible title coverage and examines a purposeful selection of ten directly relevant publications, with reading depth specified below. It is a focused design review, not a systematic review or full-text review of all 89 items. Collection attachments were not comprehensively acquired. Sources remain in Zotero and their original repositories.

## Assessment

The central expectation is well grounded as a design hypothesis: explicit, traceable relationships can help partnership members discover relevant work, compare findings, examine the scientific basis of restoration, and preserve institutional knowledge. The reviewed literature provides implemented examples of knowledge organization and integration, alongside proposals for extending these capabilities to restoration. It does not establish that this project will improve UMRR collaboration, scientific inference, or ecological outcomes. Those are different benefits requiring different evidence.

The current inductive approach, production-system anchors, preservation of source wording, expert stewardship, and immediate organizational ontology alignment fit these findings. The most useful next adjustment is to evaluate a small set of practical questions against today's workflow while refining the conceptual model. Another broad framing exercise or database build is unnecessary at this stage.

## Checking the project assumptions

| Expectation | Assessment | Practical consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listing and linking fragmented work has value before new discoveries | Supported by conceptual mapping and integration examples; preservation also remains an accepted user goal | Deliver useful, traceable inventories and question answers early |
| A graph can help a decentralized partnership work across its existing systems | Technically plausible; sustained collaboration depends on practical benefits, ownership, review effort and continued maintenance | Preserve EMMA and RF authority; test handoff and correction with intended users |
| Relationships can expose gaps and conflicting findings | Supported as an organizational capability; a missing graph link can also mean incomplete extraction | Report searched coverage and distinguish unrecorded, unreviewed, unavailable and documented absence |
| Scientific hypotheses can be inferred from reports that never state them formally | Possible with expert interpretation; not equivalent to extracting an explicit author statement | Keep source assertion, AI interpretation and expert formalization distinct; retain their derivations and review state |
| Linking a hypothesis, feature and observation evaluates the hypothesis | Insufficient by itself | Preserve study design, comparator, sampling, methods, uncertainty, confounding, analysis and transfer limits. A logical graph path is not a causal estimate |
| Automated reasoning could generate new insights | A reasonable future possibility, not a demonstrated UMRR outcome | Review the premises and evidence behind derived statements; evaluate discovery separately from retrieval |
| RAG/vector search is the foundation required for this work | Not established by this review; retrieval and explicit evidence modeling serve different purposes | Treat semantic search as an optional discovery aid. It cannot substitute for reviewed identities, relationships or scientific assessments |

## Current design and focused improvements

The [foundation](project-foundation.md), [scientific relationship analysis](../architecture/science-restoration-relationships.md), [mussel example](mussel-science-worked-example.md), and [USACE alignment](../architecture/usace-ontology-alignment.md) already establish the main direction. These improvements refine that work; they are assistant proposals, not adopted program requirements or a separate governance framework.

1. **Make each consequential link inspectable.** Preserve source passage and locator, source version, author statement versus interpretation, extractor/reviewer and revision. A paper discussing a hypothesis, a study testing it, a finding supporting it, and an assessment applying it to an HREP require distinct meanings. Reuse existing USACE provenance/lifecycle structures; administrative validity is not scientific confidence.
2. **Keep inference at the scope supported by the study.** For mussels, identify species/life stage, site, period/flow conditions, treatment/exposure, response and comparator where available. Retain unresolved fields explicitly. Break the broad habitat proposition into specific reviewable propositions when the papers support that refinement. Do not convert association to treatment effect or expert consensus to empirical confirmation.
3. **Validate cross-system meanings before merging identities.** Keep EMMA IDs, RF identifiers and source labels. Test project/feature joins and classification crosswalks against examples; do not merge by name alone. Extend the organizational model only for justified gaps and follow its existing module process later.
4. **Include contradictory and incomplete evidence.** Record null/negative findings and alternative explanations alongside support. Retain versioned criteria and assessments. Treat source coverage as part of an answer; publication counts or supporting-study majorities are not substitutes for evaluating design quality and comparability.
5. **Make curation earn its cost.** Work through existing Data Stewards, GIS staff and science reviewers. Test a handoff to someone who did not create the record, including locating its source, correcting a link and understanding the revision. Agree responsibilities through existing program workflows before scaling.

## Proposed next milestone

Continue the accepted science-side worked example. Use the existing records and obtain selected full papers where their abstracts cannot support interpretation. Prepare a small evidence table and explicit relationship list; a deployed graph database is unnecessary for this design test.

Use three practical questions as conceptual-model checks:

- What published evidence supports or challenges the proposed mussel-habitat benefit, and under what conditions?
- Which documented Steamboat Island features could plausibly engage that mechanism, and what is the basis and uncertainty of transferring the research there?
- What observations and study design would be needed to assess that expected response, and what is currently planned, collected, unavailable or unreviewed?

The second answer must keep the local benefit as ancillary. The third must preserve the distinction between not-yet-applicable post-construction performance monitoring and mussel surveys not committed under a formal objective. Neither should produce an assertion of observed local mussel success.

Have a scientist and restoration practitioner review the same answers using today's documents/Zotero/EMMA approach and the proposed linked evidence. Counterbalance the order or use comparable questions to reduce learning effects. Record answer correctness and traceability, elapsed retrieval/review time, unsupported links, missed relevant evidence and semantic changes. Check agreement between reviewers and record curation/correction time. For extraction, assess object/relationship precision and recall against independently reviewed passages, including implicit statements, negative findings and incomplete cases; hold some passages aside from model refinement. Report results by source and relationship type rather than a single reassuring accuracy percentage.

Before running this comparison, agree what improvement would justify ongoing stewardship and which errors are unacceptable for the intended use. There are no measured results or agreed numerical thresholds yet. The milestone is complete when reviewers can inspect the evidence chain, identify remaining disagreements, and assess usefulness and burden. It does not require resolving all historical sources or approving every crosswalk requirement.

## Strategic plan connection

The user identifies restoration–science integration in current UMRR strategic planning as the program motivation. The previously reviewed supplied 2015–2025 plan independently supports integration, learning and institutional knowledge; see [program evidence E1](umrr-program-review.md). Published [August 2024 UMRR meeting highlights](https://umrba.org/sites/default/files/documents/08-2024-umrr-highlights-and-action-items-summary.pdf) describe development of the 2025–2035 plan, including collaboration among program elements. These are planning records, not the adopted successor plan. The final current plan/version and exact goals should be reconciled before claiming a verified latest-plan crosswalk.

The defensible project contribution is to improve access to, continuity of, and scrutiny of the evidence connecting science and restoration. Improved ecological outcomes and program effectiveness are longer-term intended benefits, requiring scientific and program evaluation beyond graph construction.
