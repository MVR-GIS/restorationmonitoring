# Existing models, tools and programs: targeted landscape review

Reviewed 2026-11-02. This is a decision aid for the proposed UMRR science and restoration integration component, not a systematic review, procurement recommendation or technology decision.

**Scope correction, 2026-11-03:** The user clarified that general AM programs, manuals and project tracking are insufficient: the target is actual science-to-restoration-to-decision data tooling and reusable models, preferably open source. The [focused tooling watch](science-am-tooling-watch.md) supersedes this review's executive assessment, candidate priorities and proposed whole-system comparison. Retain this document as the dated broader search record; do not interpret program activity as proof of the desired database. Its standards references remain useful as component leads subject to the focused screen.

## Executive assessment

**Do not start by inventing a general adaptive-management system.** Organizations already perform much of the intended function, and several products merit a serious fit test. In particular, the USACE Everglades RECOVER program conducts monitoring and assessment to inform adaptive management; NOAA's DIVER Restoration Portal tracks restoration projects and monitoring; and Miradi supports adaptive-management planning and reports. Their existence does not establish that any can be deployed by UMRR, replace EMMA, or answer all of the proposed evidence-tracing questions. Those are testable questions, not reasons to presume a gap. [USACE RECOVER](https://www.saj.usace.army.mil/Missions/Environmental/Ecosystem-Restoration/RECOVER/); [NOAA monitoring and adaptive management manual](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/s3/2024-09/DWH-NRDA-MAM-Manual-V2.1-Sept-2024.pdf); [Miradi Share](https://www.miradishare.org/ux/).

The most defensible direction is to **follow established adaptive-management practice, map the proposed concepts to existing standards, and compare existing systems against the same worked cases before adding EMMA capabilities**. EMMA remains the user-identified likely system of record for judgments *for design purposes*, not a settled implementation choice. A favorable comparison could justify adopting another tool, integrating it with EMMA, or limiting new work to a reporting layer; an unfavorable comparison could justify targeted extensions. No outcome is predetermined.

### Scope and reading limits

The question was whether existing work can supply the project's full function or reusable parts: a practitioner can start from a research question, study, project or other item; navigate the attributed connections toward conceptual context and observations; inspect evidence, missingness and uncertainty; and record and revise distinct findings, assessments and decisions. The baseline also includes EMMA, Restoration Features GIS and the local USACE ontology. An old project with no recorded monitoring task must still be analyzable from other evidence.

This review supplements the earlier [knowledge-graph literature assessment](knowledge-graph-literature-assessment.md). On 2026-11-02, targeted web searches covered restoration adaptive-management portals, conservation planning and evidence tools, USACE/USGS and NOAA programs, observation and provenance standards, and ecological evidence models. Official program, standards, project and vendor documentation and a restoration knowledge-graph paper were prioritized. Source pages and selected accessible report passages were reviewed; systems were **not** installed, exercised, inspected under authenticated access, or surveyed comprehensively. Vendor documentation demonstrates documented features, not independently verified fitness. Access, hosting, ownership, pricing, security, interfaces and maintenance need confirmation. Search is English-language and selective; absence from this review is not evidence a system does not exist.

## 1. Does someone already do this?

| Candidate | What the cited material establishes | What remains to decide for UMRR |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **USACE CERP RECOVER**, Everglades | An interagency team coordinates evaluation, assessment, planning and integration. Its monitoring and assessment work supports restoration performance evaluation and adaptive-management recommendations. This is an especially close **organizational** precedent within USACE. [RECOVER](https://www.saj.usace.army.mil/Missions/Environmental/Ecosystem-Restoration/RECOVER/); [adaptive assessment and monitoring](https://www.saj.usace.army.mil/About/Congressional-Fact-Sheets-2025/CERP-Adaptive-Assessment-Monitoring-C/). | Ask how hypotheses, evidence, competing interpretations, decisions and revisions are recorded, connected and found; request sample workflows, data dictionaries and transferable software. The sources do not establish a reusable EMMA replacement. CERP's program-specific obligations are not UMRR requirements. |
| **NOAA DIVER Restoration Portal / Explorer** | NOAA describes centralized project and monitoring information, activities, accomplishments and reporting for Deepwater Horizon restoration. A USGS cross-trustee monitoring group describes portal input and interactive reporting. [NOAA MAM manual](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/s3/2024-09/DWH-NRDA-MAM-Manual-V2.1-Sept-2024.pdf); [USGS cross-TIG work](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/wetland-and-aquatic-research-center/science/natural-resource-damage-and-assessment-nrda); [DIVER](https://diver.orr.noaa.gov/). | A credible candidate for substantial overlap, and potentially more than a source of inspiration. Verify whether external organizations can use or deploy it; interfaces, licensing and access; integration with EMMA and GIS; and whether it captures and navigates question-to-evidence-to-authored-judgment links, including revisions. Public documentation reviewed does not establish those answers. |
| **Miradi / Miradi Share** | Conservation planning software supports planning, adaptive-management project information, collaboration and reports. Its documentation describes project/program report documents and a read-only project API with plan and permission conditions. Desktop client source is public; that alone does not establish the hosting or licensing terms of the Share service. [Product](https://www.miradishare.org/ux/); [reports](https://help.miradishare.org/hc/en-us/articles/6665997837083-Report-Documents-in-Projects-Programs-Overview); [API](https://help.miradishare.org/hc/en-us/articles/7651985079963-Miradi-API); [desktop source](https://github.com/fosonline/miradi-client). | Test the whole workflow, not just diagrams: cross-project discovery, evidence attribution, conflicting and revised judgments, and GIS/EMMA integration. Confirm current service terms, costs, export, deployment and API permissions. A report document generated from a project is not itself proof of dynamic multi-entry evidence traversal. |
| **Australian MERIT and EMSA** | A national government system supports environmental project monitoring, evaluation and reporting; EMSA provides a related ecological monitoring approach. [MERIT](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/land/natural-heritage-trust/monitoring-reporting/merit); [EMSA](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/land/natural-heritage-trust/monitoring-reporting/emsa). | Learn from the national program's governance, data structures and evaluation workflow. Reuse outside its program, software availability and the proposed detailed provenance and discovery capabilities are unverified. |
| **Conservation Evidence** | Curates and summarizes published evidence about conservation interventions; its toolkit helps practitioners find and use evidence. [About](https://about.conservationevidence.com/about/); [toolkit](https://conservationevidence.com/images/Tool%20images/Conservation%20Evidence%20Toolkit%20v1.0%2015-01-2025.pdf). | An important complement for external evidence and synthesis. Verify suitability and reuse terms for particular questions. Its existence does not establish access to UMRR's institutional records or practitioner-authored local decisions. |
| **USGS Land Treatment Digital Library** | A USGS/BLM partnership maintains an extensive searchable record of land treatments and supporting information. Its older published manual describes linked monitoring documents. [USGS library](https://www.usgs.gov/tools/land-treatment-digital-library); [2013 manual](https://pubs.usgs.gov/ds/749/data/ds749_LTDL%20User%27s%20Manual%20and%20Installation%20Instructions%20Arc10.1.pdf). | A strong precedent for treatment histories, GIS and long-term curation. Ask about current monitoring, data models, evidence integration and reuse; do not treat the 2013 manual as a description of today's complete system. |

**Answer to the replacement question:** yes, comparable adaptive-management work is already being performed, and there are software products worth considering as potential alternatives. The available public evidence does **not** establish that a single identified offering currently covers UMRR's full combination of local records, both-direction evidence discovery, attributed and revisable judgments, and institutional access. Nor does it establish that any candidate fails those tests. Investigate the closest candidates before committing to custom functionality.

## 2. Standards and models to follow or test

The existing [USACE ontology alignment](../architecture/usace-ontology-alignment.md) is the starting point. The following are **alignment candidates**, not a requirement to implement RDF, adopt every term or publish a new ontology. Compare meaning and relationships against actual UMRR examples before selecting a vocabulary.

| Need | Existing model or standard | Practical use and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observations, samples and procedures | [W3C SOSA/SSN 2017 recommendation](https://www.w3.org/TR/2017/REC-vocab-ssn-20171019/) | Compare observation, feature of interest, observed property, procedure, sample and result to the study/monitoring model. The separate [SSN 2023 edition](https://www.w3.org/TR/vocab-ssn-2023/) is a **working draft** as reviewed; check status and compatibility before relying on its additions. Do not restrict biological observations to hydrology-specific subclasses. |
| Source and activity lineage | [W3C PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/) | Map entities, activities, agents and qualified derivation to document extraction, analysis and attribution. Provenance of a source alone does not mean its content supports a particular scientific claim. |
| Claims and supporting or conflicting evidence | [SEPIO LinkML information model](https://github.com/sepio-framework/sepio-linkml) | Test the distinction among assertion, evidence line, source and attribution against findings and assessments. Originates in another scientific community; suitability, coverage and maintenance for UMRR require review. The [older OWL repository](https://github.com/monarch-initiative/SEPIO-ontology) notes divergence from the LinkML model, so version and model choice matter. |
| Spatial features and relations | [OGC GeoSPARQL 1.1](https://docs.ogc.org/is/22-047r1/22-047r1.html) | Compare feature/geometry and spatial-relation semantics with Restoration Features and the USACE ontology. Record inputs, method, tolerance and time when a spatial inference matters; a spatial standard does not establish identity or causation. |
| Dataset discovery and metadata | [W3C DCAT 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/vocab-dcat-3/); [Ecological Metadata Language](https://eml.ecoinformatics.org/eml-ecological-metadata-language) | DCAT helps describe catalogs, datasets and distributions; EML describes ecological datasets, methods and coverage. Neither substitutes for observation records or evidence-linked judgments. For USGS releases, check applicable [USGS metadata requirements](https://www.usgs.gov/sciencebase-instructions-and-documentation/sciencebase-data-release-checklist); EML is not presumed to satisfy them by itself. |
| Biodiversity records | [Darwin Core](https://dwc.tdwg.org/schemas/) | Compare event, occurrence and measurement terms where biodiversity data are relevant; this is not a complete program or decision model. |
| Ecological survey design | [TERN conceptual information model](https://linkeddata.tern.org.au/information-models/tern-ontology/conceptual-information-model) | Review survey, event and observation patterns alongside SOSA/SSN; evaluate against local studies rather than assuming the profile covers restoration decisions. |
| Adaptive-management process | [Conservation Standards](https://www.conservationstandards.org/download-cs/) | Use established planning, monitoring, evaluation and learning concepts to challenge the proposed workflow. This is a management framework, not a complete database exchange schema. |

**Near-term mapping:** compare the existing local ontology plus SOSA/SSN for a real observation; PROV-O for its source and analysis lineage; and SEPIO-style assertion/evidence separation for a hypothetical judgment. Use terms where their meaning fits; document mismatches, version and local extensions. A standard alignment is not proof that a model or tool satisfies the user's reporting requirements.

### Existing USGS data infrastructure is changing

The [ScienceBase modernization FAQ](https://www.usgs.gov/sciencebase-instructions-and-documentation/sciencebase-modernization-faqs) describes a transition and anticipated API changes; the [winter 2026 update](https://www.usgs.gov/sciencebase-instructions-and-documentation/sciencebase-updates-winter-2026) discusses preserving DOIs while replacing ScienceBase identifiers. Treat dates as planning information subject to change. Where a DOI actually exists, retain it alongside current source identifiers and aliases, and plan to reconcile migrated records rather than embedding assumptions about permanent ScienceBase URLs or APIs. Do not assume every item or attachment has a DOI.

## 3. Research groups and possible coordination

These are leads for the user and program to evaluate, not contacts already made or assertions of willingness to collaborate.

1. **USACE RECOVER**: ask for a demonstration of their evidence-to-assessment-to-decision practice, data lineage and decision documentation, and which artifacts or tools can transfer to UMRR. Start with the [RECOVER program](https://www.saj.usace.army.mil/Missions/Environmental/Ecosystem-Restoration/RECOVER/) and its [adaptive-management resources](https://www.saj.usace.army.mil/RECOVER/Adaptive_Mgmt/).
2. **NOAA DIVER and the Deepwater Horizon cross-TIG monitoring community**: request a project-and-question walkthrough, data dictionary, API/export and reuse/hosting terms, and how monitoring results and judgments are linked. [DIVER](https://diver.orr.noaa.gov/); [USGS cross-TIG work](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/wetland-and-aquatic-research-center/science/natural-resource-damage-and-assessment-nrda).
3. **USGS Land Treatment Digital Library team**: compare treatment identity, historical records, spatial change and links to monitoring and evidence. The [USGS project page](https://www.usgs.gov/tools/land-treatment-digital-library) identifies the team and relevant context.
4. **Conservation Measures Partnership and Miradi**: compare results chains, assessment documentation, program-wide evidence navigation, exports and integration conditions. [Conservation Standards](https://www.conservationstandards.org/download-cs/); [Miradi](https://www.miradishare.org/ux/).
5. **Conservation Evidence and restoration knowledge-graph researchers**: discuss evidence synthesis, claim-level provenance and distinguishing local evidence from general intervention knowledge. [Conservation Evidence](https://about.conservationevidence.com/about/); [Heger et al., restoration knowledge-graph proposal](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/rec.13676); [research group and publications](https://www.hi-knowledge.org/links-and-publications). The paper is a research proposal, not evidence that a ready-to-deploy UMRR system exists.
6. **MERLIN river restoration and Australian MERIT/EMSA teams**: compare cross-site assessment, indicators, monitoring and reporting. [MERLIN deliverables](https://www.project-merlin.eu/deliverables.html); [MERIT](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/land/natural-heritage-trust/monitoring-reporting/merit); [EMSA](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/land/natural-heritage-trust/monitoring-reporting/emsa).

Before approaching outside groups, reconcile these leads with current UMRR and USGS work and existing relationships. Coordination is a future program choice; this review did not request information or contact anyone.

## Recommended next step: one comparative fit test

Use the same small, source-attributed fixture for **EMMA plus a prospective reporting layer**, DIVER and Miradi; add RECOVER's workflow or software if its team identifies a transferable candidate. This is a desk comparison until access and a demonstration are available. Score each requirement as **documented, demonstrated, not supported, or not yet verified**, citing the evidence and software version; do not equate an unverified feature with an absent feature.

1. Start independently from Bertom's WQ-39 question, the 2014 survey and the project. Can the same relevant sources and connections be found in both directions, and can a reader drill down to their provenance?
2. Include the candidate rock-habitat feature and a spatial link with method and time. Can the system distinguish a proposed relation from a verified identity or inference?
3. Include an explicitly **hypothetical**, practitioner-authored finding that evidence is insufficient, a separate assessment, a decision or deferral, and a subsequent revision. Can the system preserve authorship, date, competing interpretations, supporting and contrary evidence, and the previous state?
4. Omit a recorded monitoring task and a usable performance criterion where they are genuinely unavailable. Can a user still discover and analyze historical evidence without fabricating records or presenting absence as proof of no evidence?
5. Check access and deployment, external-source links, exports and APIs, current licensing/cost, security, roles, audit history, cross-system identifiers and geometry, preservation, maintenance responsibility and the time required to curate the case. Identify who controls and can revise the judgments.

Use synthetic judgments and source-supported existing case details; do not create production records or infer a project-success result. Compare demonstrated capabilities, not marketing labels, and record outstanding questions for a future program-led conversation. The result should support a decision among adopting, integrating, configuring, or building only the missing capability.
