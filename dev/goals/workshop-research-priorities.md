## How to interpret the rankings

The main header defines the measure as **“Rank average of weighted agency averages (1=highest priority, 49=lowest priority)”**. Reach labels report Upper Impounded `n=13`, Lower Impounded `n=9`, Unimpounded `n=6`, Illinois River `n=2`. The workbook does not define what `n` counts, identify the included agencies/participants, provide raw ratings or weights, or describe how weighting was applied. Do not describe these as confirmed participant sample sizes or independently reproduced weighted scores.

All ranks are stored values; no worksheet formulas were found. The observed tied ranks, including 2.5, 18.5 and 37.5, are retained. Each reach's 49 ranks sums to 1,225, consistent with the complete rank positions 1–49 and average-rank ties; this is a structural check, not verification of the unavailable weighting calculation. Lower numbers indicate greater recorded priority. The differences between ranks do not measure differences in scientific importance, evidence strength or expected return on investment.

There is no supplied system-wide composite ranking. This review does not average reach ranks, weight them by the unexplained `n`, or claim a consensus order across the program.

## Implications for the conceptual model and reports

The following are assistant proposals grounded in this new source, not approved classes, tables or organizational ontology terms:

1. **Anchor the learning agenda in source question records.** Preserve original question wording and goal/objective groupings. Broad or multipart questions can motivate several explicit hypotheses or studies; do not turn a question automatically into a confirmed hypothesis.
2. **Keep priority assessments separate from questions and findings.** A ranking belongs to a question, reach and particular assessment/workshop version, with its method, reported `n` and missing protocol information. A later ranking should preserve earlier assessments. Priority is not scientific confidence or administrative validity.
3. **Represent learning opportunities explicitly.** A question/project association can record the observed workbook mark, source cell, proposed meaning and review history. Separately identify a relevant physical feature, study design, observations and analytical evidence before claiming that an HREP tested or answered the question.
4. **Make reporting answer management questions.** Show priority question → existing studies/findings → relevant HREPs/features → available or missing evidence → next learning opportunity. Display reach-specific ranks and source coverage alongside the answer, without manufacturing a single program score.
5. **Reuse existing organizational concepts.** Apply the [USACE baseline](../architecture/usace-ontology-alignment.md) for document/provenance, spatial and temporal context and observations. Investigate scientific-question, priority-assessment and applicability gaps through the established extension process; this review does not codify a module.

### Consequences for the current pilot and FY27 proposal

The [mussel science example](mussel-science-worked-example.md) is relevant to WQ-39 and potentially WQ-05/WQ-40, with a separate adverse-response question in WQ-37. These are candidate semantic connections for review, not adopted equivalences between SC-08 and a workshop question. Steamboat Island appears on the Lower Impounded sheet, where WQ-39 ranks 16; its blank matrix column does not establish a studied relationship.

Retain mussels/Steamboat as the worked example already selected by the user. The rankings also make reforestation a strong candidate for a later contrasting example, subject to program priorities, available evidence and reviewer capacity. Do not silently substitute a new pilot theme or promise two themes within the existing one-theme budget.

The FY27 proposal should begin by confirming priorities and choosing learning opportunities from this existing workshop agenda, not conducting a new ranking exercise by default. The proposed 20-artifact/two-HREP pilot remains a bounded test of whether EMMA can operationalize that agenda. The ranked questions can guide source selection and demonstrate why a report is useful to a manager, while feasibility, coverage, costs and staff judgment still govern selection.
