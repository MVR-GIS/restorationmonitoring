# FWWG practitioner discovery: prompts for later

- Recorded: 2026-11-02. Status: deferred until FWWG members have capacity; no interviews or partner validation conducted.
- Purpose: learn how practitioners expect to use the developing restoration-science research framework after the current prioritization exercise. The user expects the group may begin examining specific questions in the next couple of months; timing is an expectation, not a confirmed schedule.
- Basis: [FWWG and draft strategic-plan review](strategic-plan-fwwg-review.md). These prompts are analyst proposals informed by the initiative, not claims about the group's actual decisions or approved workflow.

## Conversations to return to

1. **Selecting an opportunity.** When considering a priority uncertainty, what would you need to see to decide whether a restoration project offers a useful learning opportunity? What makes it promising, uncertain or unsuitable?
2. **Designing and coordinating a study.** Once a project looks promising, what do you need to understand about the intervention, ecological response, existing studies, monitoring, location, timing and potential partners? What coordination already happens?
3. **Learning from prior work.** When reviewing earlier projects or studies, which evidence and contextual differences make a finding useful for a new design? Which missing details change what you can conclude?
4. **Communicating a recommendation.** What would you show colleagues to explain a potential opportunity, its uncertainty and the decision or next action under consideration?

For each account, ask about a real recent example: the person's role and situation, decision or question, existing process and collaborators, evidence sought, difficulties, and what happened next. Ask what a useful result would let them do. Avoid asking them to endorse a proposed schema or assume that a report is the right tool.

## Candidate stories, not attributed to practitioners

- A working-group participant comparing opportunities may need to see a question, reach context, candidate projects, available evidence and gaps together before recommending a closer review.
- A project biologist may need to connect a proposed practice and implementation schedule with a study's response, methods and partner responsibilities before assessing feasibility.
- A researcher may need to compare how past studies defined location, substrate, response and inference before deciding whether a prior finding is applicable.

Each story requires direct practitioner review. No interview, meeting, outreach or schedule is authorized by this note. Use the conceptual-model and visualization work to make later discussions concrete without presenting hypothetical preferences as FWWG requirements.
