## View A: begin with WQ-39

1. Recover the question, its source and each reach-specific rank. Include the workbook's Bertom mark with its unexplained semantics; record the analyst's topical rationale separately.
2. From that candidate association, expose Bertom's historical objective and channel, with project identity, source version and unresolved GIS match. This is a path to potentially relevant evidence, not proof of a study designed to answer WQ-39.
3. Show the report-described 2014 survey and its scoped results through their own project/channel and survey-to-finding evidence. The mark does not supply those links or establish scientific relevance at every scale.
4. Show missing coverage: only this bounded case and supplied sources were reviewed; absence of additional projects or research is not established. No FWWG opportunity selection or question-level answer is recorded.

## View B: begin with the 2014 survey

1. Recover the report's purpose, methods, sites and comparison areas; show the report and collection dates separately. Do not supply WQ-39 as the survey's original motivation without evidence.
2. Follow the report's project and habitat-channel context back to historical objectives and targets, retaining the separate candidate crosswalk to UMRRDB/GIS records. A survey site is not automatically a GIS feature.
3. Follow the survey report to the channel and site findings, retaining mixed methods and the inconclusive downstream interpretation. Source-reported results are discoverable even though individual measurements were not recovered and no EMMA task was recorded.
4. WQ-39 may appear as a **later candidate relevance** through the workbook association and analyst rationale. Reversing the navigation direction does not change that link into a source-stated research purpose or causal relationship.

## View C: begin with the Bertom project

1. Recover the UMRRDB project label/ID and candidate identity evidence, then the historical project objectives, target statements and channel with their separate source locators. The same project number does not establish EMMA identity semantics.
2. Present the 2014 survey and reported findings as later project evidence, including the absent EMMA task and unverified feature crosswalk. Neither missing task nor missing raw data means the survey did not occur.
3. Offer WQ-39 as a workbook-marked and analyst-relevant candidate, explicitly not a confirmed project study question. Reach rank is an assessment of the question in that workbook, not an assessment of the project.
4. Show no reviewed formal target-attainment judgment or later management decision in the bounded case; do not equate this search result with proof that none exists. The [durable-judgment model](../architecture/durable-assessment-and-decision-model.md) proposes how future practitioners could record such judgments when warranted.

## Model test outcome

The three views recover the same question, project, objectives, channel, survey and report finding without requiring the same ordering or an invented task. They emphasize different initial context while preserving each relationship's direction, attribution and limitations. The exercise exposes a need for source-level report findings, analyst relevance, future practitioner-authored assessments, and decisions to be individually discoverable and distinguishable; it does **not** establish that live systems contain those records or that a query engine can traverse them today.

Next prototype the shared evidence set and independently selectable entry views, including path explanations and missing coverage. Test contradictions, multiple paths and cycles before claiming repeatable dynamic retrieval. Separately review the proposed judgment concepts with appropriate practitioners when they have capacity.
