## Attributed connections

**Priority to case — candidate interpretation.** WQ-39 concerns substrate properties for mussels, other benthos and fish spawning; the workbook records an unexplained project association mark. The project and survey concern rock substrate and mussels, so they are relevant to the question. This does not mean the 2014 study was designed to answer the later workshop question or that the question is resolved. See the [workshop review](workshop-research-priorities.md) for rank and protocol limits.

**Objective and target — documented planning.** The DPR table lists increasing rock substrate aquatic habitat and establishing a mussel bed under the aquatic-habitat goal, with respective enhancement-potential targets of 10,000 square yards and 10 mussels per square yard. The PER table identifies the same values as year-50 targets and associates both objectives with the fish and mussel rock habitat alternative and rock habitat channel. These historical target statements are candidates for later criterion review, not verified EMMA performance-criterion records or an assessment of attainment. The FY27 workbook reproduces the objective–feature association but leaves its master performance-criteria cells blank.

**Practice — planned and later described.** DPR p. 14 describes a proposed 1,500-foot channel divided into seven sections with different rock material and fish structures. The 2015 survey report pp. 2–3 describes a constructed rock-lined channel with seven sections and different substrates. Restoration Features snapshot project ID 65 has seven `RESTORATION.Revetment` records named Rock Habitat Channel Segments 1–7, IDs 6723–6729, marked `USACE Built`. The project number matches the UMRRDB Bertom row and compilation. Naming, number and segment structure provide a strong candidate correspondence; they do not establish EMMA project-ID equivalence or individually validate segment boundaries, geometry, feature identity or as-built state at survey time. The same feature ID may occur in another class; retain class and source version.

**Monitoring — historical plan and event.** The PER table lists an area mussel survey for the mussel-bed objective. Its p. 1 says mussel surveys had not been completed and were scheduled for a later addendum. The 2015 report says five-year surveys were planned but not conducted until 2014; the survey took place 7–9 October 2014 and sampled the habitat channel, control channel and downstream areas. This is report evidence of an actual event, not an EMMA task or event record. Sampling methods varied by location: segments 1, 3 and 4 received qualitative searches only, while segment 6 received quantitative substrate quadrats only; see report pp. 3–4 for the reasons and complete methods. A site, segment, sample, observation and resulting analysis are distinct references.

**Result and interpretation — scoped report findings.** The report p. 7 states that 40 live mussels representing 11 species were collected in the habitat channel, combining its reported survey methods. It reports site-specific quantitative densities, including 4.8/m² at site 5 and 4.0/m² at site 6. On pp. 8–9 the author interprets apparent colonization in some substrates and discusses possible mechanisms; the report calls the effect on downstream Coalpit Slough inconclusive. Different methods and units, sampling coverage, temporal and spatial scope and the target's intended evaluation method must be reconciled before comparing these results to the historical year-50 target. No criterion evaluation, project-success judgment or causal effect is established by this review.

Do not infer a task from the historical survey or create new identifiers or obligations. This case is nevertheless useful for reviewing how an attributed link can preserve a historical event despite a missing operational task record.

## Analysis with incomplete historical tracking

The user explained that EMMA development deliberately did not require monitoring-task entries for older completed projects with no future monitoring planned. Some evidence of completed work was never recorded and may be difficult or impossible to recover. This is a user-reported system decision and general historical limitation; for Bertom, the specific confirmed fact is that EMMA has no recorded monitoring tasks. It does not establish whether all Bertom raw data or other records survive.

An analyst can still follow this source-backed route: WQ-39 candidate relevance → historical mussel-bed objective and channel → report-described 2014 survey → reported channel findings, with each arrow carrying its own attribution and qualification. The survey report supplies date, comparison areas and methods; it does not require an invented EMMA task to be discoverable. This supports a descriptive review of what the report says, subject to its sampling design and methods. The absence of recovered raw data should be reported as an availability gap, not turned into a claim that data are permanently lost.

Different questions require different evidence. A source-located summary of the report's collected mussels can be used as such. Reproducing its calculations requires the underlying observations and methods at the necessary detail. Matching a sampled segment to a particular GIS polygon requires a documented crosswalk or suitable spatial inputs and time alignment. Comparing survey results with the historical target requires a defensible matching unit, area, timing, method and evaluation rule. The current evidence does not establish those latter steps. A missing prerequisite limits the specific analysis or inference that needs it, not the ability to find or describe the report's supported results.

For this case, carry these distinct states in the narrative: EMMA task **not recorded** (user-confirmed); survey **performed** (report evidence); raw observations and survey geometry **not located in this review**, not proven nonexistent; GIS segment identity **candidate, unverified**; target assessment **not established**. These are descriptive working distinctions, not a prescribed status vocabulary or production workflow.
